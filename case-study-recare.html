Recare: Preventing Refund and Credit Leakage

This wasn't a refund-UI polish job. It was about stopping money from leaking out the door because agents couldn't see what had already been paid out.

Impact

One of our major businesses was bleeding close to $1M a year on incorrect refunds and credits. It wasn't people gaming the system: agents just didn't have the context they needed at the exact moment they were about to issue a refund.

We redesigned Recare to put that context front and center, and added guardrails that stopped further refunds or credits once an order had already been overpaid.

~$1M

Guardrails

Refunds and credits blocked once an order is fully compensated

The problem

The old refund screen showed agents just enough to issue a refund: nothing about what had already been refunded or credited on that order or its individual items.

Which meant an agent could easily issue a full refund without ever knowing part of the order was already compensated.

Say an order was worth $100 and had already gotten a $50 credit; the right refund is $50. Without that context in front of them, an agent could refund the full $100 without realizing it, and the customer just got paid twice.

Multiply that across enough orders, and it adds up fast.

Reframing the experience

We tackled it as a progressive set of safeguards, each one a little firmer than the last.

1. Surface refund and credit history

We put prior refund and credit history right into the refund workflow itself. Before issuing anything, an agent could see at a glance whether:

The order had already gotten a refund

The order had already gotten a credit

A specific item had already been refunded or credited

The idea was pretty simple: add friction, but only exactly where it's useful. Instead of expecting agents to go dig through the system on their own, we put the information right in front of them at the moment they were about to make a financial call.

It gave agents a beat to pause and ask: has this customer already been made whole for this?

Refund history: screenshot

2. Make overpayment visible

We also added a clear call-out whenever the system caught an order or item that had already been compensated past what it was eligible for. That turned what used to be invisible into something you couldn't miss.

Issue refund→discover context later
Understand compensation history→make an informed decision

3. Move from warnings to guardrails

For orders or items already overpaid, we went further still. As a product team, we made the call that these cases just shouldn't be allowed to have another refund or credit go through, full stop.

That was the real shift. We stopped trusting a warning to be read correctly and just built the rule into the product itself.

From information to prevention

What the redesign actually landed on was a ladder of safeguards:

History→ Awareness→ Warning→ Prevention

First we gave agents the context they were missing, then we called out overpayment when it showed up, and finally we blocked further financial action once the system knew compensation had already gone past what was owed.

Somewhere in that process, Recare stopped being just a transactional refund tool and became an actual financial control point, one that helps agents make better calls while protecting the business from repeat refunds, credits, and accidental overpayment.

From a transactional refund tool to a financial control point.
