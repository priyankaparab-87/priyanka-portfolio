The challenge

"Where is my order?" (WISMO) accounted for roughly 50% of customer-care inquiries, yet the existing Order Details experience made answering it unnecessarily difficult.

Agents had to navigate across three different platforms, piece together information from different sections, and scroll extensively through the page. This became particularly painful for complex orders with multiple items.

At the same time, the experience served very different personas, from customer-care agents to support, engineering and production teams, each requiring different levels of information and investigation.

The opportunity was to turn Order Details from a place that stored information into a place where users could immediately understand what was happening with an order.

Reframing the experience

We rebuilt the experience around three principles:

1. Make the order journey immediately understandable

We introduced an Order Timeline that surfaced the most important events in the order lifecycle.

Instead of searching through multiple systems, agents could now see the progression of an order in one place and quickly answer the customer's question.

Order Timeline — screenshot

2. Reduce scrolling and improve discovery

The existing horizontal item cards made complex orders difficult to scan.

We redesigned them as vertical cards, allowing more items to be visible within the available viewport. We also improved artwork and item previews and introduced clearer navigation across:

Items Shipping Billing Financials Customer Information Events

Related information was grouped together so agents could find what they needed without constantly navigating the page.

Vertical item cards — screenshot

3. Bring order and customer context together

We introduced a dedicated Pricing card with a detailed financial breakdown, making order-level refunds, credits and payment investigations easier.

We also brought customer metadata into Order Details, eliminating the need to open another system to understand the customer, their tier and the appropriate recovery action.

Agents could now make decisions with order and customer context in one place.

Pricing card & customer metadata — screenshot

Designing for more than customer care

As we explored the broader ecosystem, we found that Order Details was also critical for support, engineering and production teams.

Every order generates a large number of business-specific lifecycle events. Simply exposing them wasn't enough. We needed to make them understandable and actionable.

We introduced event categories such as:

Key Events Warnings Updates Payment Events Lifecycle Events

This made the event history easier for non-technical users to understand while preserving the depth needed for technical investigation.

We also introduced access to order-level and event-level JSON.

Order-level JSON Event-level JSON

Support and engineering teams could investigate questions such as:

Why has an order been stuck in a particular state?

Has it exceeded its expected SLA?

Why did an action return a 404?

What data was associated with the event when the problem occurred?

Investigation could now begin directly within Order Details instead of requiring teams to move between multiple tools.

Designing across domains and at scale

The complexity wasn't just in the interface. The experience needed to work across multiple businesses, order lifecycles, technical systems and stakeholder groups.

Business teams cared about customer resolution. Support teams needed diagnosis. Engineering needed access to underlying data. Each had different definitions of what mattered.

A significant part of the design leadership involved aligning these cross-domain stakeholders around a shared experience, while avoiding solutions that only worked for one business or workflow.

We focused on building a scalable information architecture and flexible interaction model that could accommodate new events, APIs, business requirements and personas over time.

The goal wasn't to design for a single order lifecycle. It was to create a foundation that could evolve with the platform.

The outcome

What began as a WISMO problem became a broader systems-design opportunity.

Order Details evolved from a fragmented information screen into a shared operational tool for understanding, resolving and investigating orders.

One order. One source of truth. Multiple teams. One scalable experience.
