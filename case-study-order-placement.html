Case study — Order Placement

Order Placement: From Cart to Sales Workflow

Rebuilding Order Placement as an end-to-end sales workflow — from customer lookup to order conversion — rather than a cart redesign.

AHT ↓ Consolidating a fragmented cart workflow into one flow drove a massive reduction in Average Handle Time
50%+ Of all orders were reorders — redesigned around this dominant behavior

The challenge

Order Placement had evolved into more than a traditional cart. For sales agents, a cart could also represent a quote, with the eventual goal of converting that quote into an order. This meant the experience needed to support the full journey from identifying the customer to building a customized purchase and completing payment.

The existing experience required agents to navigate across fragmented workflows, making a relatively simple sales interaction unnecessarily complex.

1. Start with the customer

We first simplified customer lookup, making it faster for agents to identify and select the right customer before beginning the order.

This created a clear starting point for the workflow and established the customer context that could then power the rest of the experience.

2. Design around reorders

Data showed that more than 50% of orders were reorders. Instead of treating every order as a new purchase, we redesigned the experience around this dominant behavior.

Previous customer orders were surfaced prominently, allowing agents to quickly find an existing product and use it as the starting point for a new order.

This significantly reduced the effort required to place repeat orders and resulted in a substantial reduction in AHT.

3. Bring customization into Add to Cart

The products being sold often required significant customization, so simply adding an existing product to a cart was not enough. We built a richer Add to Cart workflow that brought the key sales actions together:

Customize products Apply discounts Add or edit artwork Reuse artwork from previous orders Build on existing products

This transformed Add to Cart from a transactional action into a core part of the sales workflow.

4. Build for the future of payments

Payment was another critical part of the journey. While the underlying payment APIs were not yet ready, we designed the UX with future extensibility in mind.

Rather than optimizing for a single payment method, we created a flexible structure that could accommodate multiple payment methods as the APIs matured.

This allowed us to establish the interaction model early while keeping the experience scalable for future payment capabilities.

The fundamental shift was treating Order Placement as an end-to-end sales workflow, rather than a collection of cart features. By connecting customer context, reorder behavior, customization, discounts, artwork management, and payment into one coherent experience, we reduced the cognitive and operational load on sales agents while creating a scalable foundation for future order placement capabilities.
