Case study — Recare

Recare: Preventing Refund and Credit Leakage

Redesigning Recare to surface compensation history upfront and introduce guardrails that prevent financial leakage — not just a refund UI improvement.

~$1M Annual leakage from incorrect refunds and credits
Guardrails Refunds and credits blocked once an order is fully compensated

The problem

The existing refund experience showed agents only the information required to issue a refund. It did not provide enough historical context about what had already been refunded or credited against the order or individual items.

As a result, agents could issue a full refund without knowing that part of the order had already been compensated.

For example, if an order was worth $100 and had already received a $50 credit, the appropriate refund would be $50. Without that context, agents could inadvertently refund the full $100, effectively overpaying the customer.

At scale, this created significant financial leakage.

1. Surface refund and credit history

We introduced previous refund and credit history directly into the refund workflow. Before issuing a refund, agents could immediately see whether the order had previously received a refund or credit, or whether an individual item had already been refunded or credited.

The goal was deliberately simple: introduce the right amount of friction at the right moment. Instead of expecting agents to investigate the order across different parts of the system, we surfaced the information exactly when they were about to make a financial decision.

This gave agents an opportunity to pause and ask: has this customer already been compensated for this issue?

2. Make overpayment visible

We also introduced clear overpayment call-outs when the system detected that an order or item had already received more compensation than it was eligible for. This turned what was previously hidden financial context into an explicit signal for the agent.

The experience moved from issuing a refund and discovering context later, to understanding compensation history first and making an informed decision.

3. Move from warnings to guardrails

For cases where an order or item was already overpaid, we went one step further. As a product team, we decided that these cases should be blocked from further Recare actions, specifically refunds and credits.

This was an important shift in our approach. Rather than relying entirely on agents to interpret warnings correctly, we built the business rule directly into the product.

The redesign established a progressive set of safeguards — history, awareness, warning, and prevention. We first gave agents the context they were missing, then highlighted potential overpayment, and finally prevented additional financial actions when the system determined that compensation had already exceeded the eligible amount. This transformed Recare from a transactional refund tool into a financial control point, helping agents make better decisions while protecting the business from repeat refunds, credits, and accidental overpayment.
