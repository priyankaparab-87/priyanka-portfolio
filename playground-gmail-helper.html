Chrome Gmail helper extension for seniors

Gmail, simplified for the people who need it most.

Demo recording: Chrome Gmail helper extension for seniors

We often celebrate digital products for making life easier. But easier for whom?

Most interfaces are designed around users who already understand how things work. For seniors, even simple tasks like finding an email, attaching a file, or locating a contact can create anxiety and uncertainty. The problem isn't capability. It's that the interface assumes too much.

I saw this firsthand at home. My parents would regularly come to me for help with basic Gmail tasks: finding an old email, attaching a file, or figuring out which contact to use.

So I built a small Gmail companion widget that surfaced these everyday tasks through a much simpler interface.

I had very limited coding experience, but with a lot of help from Claude, I was able to turn the idea into a working prototype and test it in the context that mattered most: at home.

The outcome was surprisingly simple. My mom started coming to me far less often for Gmail help.

Does it solve the problem?

Definitely not.

It provides a temporary layer of guidance over an interface that wasn't designed with this audience in mind. But sometimes a small intervention can make technology feel a little less intimidating.

What would it look like if we designed the experience differently from the ground up?

Rather than adding more guidance to increasingly complex interfaces, perhaps we should remove the complexity altogether.

An agentic Gmail experience could reduce the interface to a handful of intent-driven touchpoints: Find an email. Send a file. Find a contact.

Following Dieter Rams' principle of taking away everything unnecessary until there is nothing left to remove, the goal isn't to create a better Gmail interface.

It's to create an experience where there is simply less interface to figure out.

And that, to me, is where the opportunity gets interesting.
